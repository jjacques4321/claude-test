## 5. Objections to answer (usable as body copy, not just internal notes)

- **"Don't put oil on your scalp."** Answer: a few drops on a clean
  scalp, worked in, two or three times a week. It doesn't replace washing.
- **"Don't oil if you have dandruff."** Answer: agree. PFA is for dry,
  tight scalps that flake from dryness — not dandruff. Greasy yellow
  flakes, redness, or sores need a dermatologist, not an oil. (Good
  trust-building line: redirecting a genuine medical case to a
  dermatologist rather than overselling the product.)
- **"Oils don't grow hair."** Answer: agree, directly. PFA promises
  moisture, slip, shine, and softness — not growth. This honest-
  disagreement framing is itself a strong, differentiated hook
  (most competitors imply growth; PFA can stand out by explicitly not
  claiming it).
- **"Will it burn?"** Answer: no peppermint, no menthol, about 1%
  essential oils.

## 6. Claim rules (supplement to `nia-voice-validation.md`'s DO/DON'T table)

**Never say** (additional banned phrases beyond the other file's list):
"regrows," "grows edges back," "stops hair loss," "dandruff" or
"antidandruff" (stronger than the other file's "treats dandruff" — this
one bans the bare word), "relieves itching," "clinically proven," "works
like minoxidil," "rosemary is proven to grow hair." The FDA treats growth
and dandruff claims as drug claims.

**OK to say**: moisture, shine, softness, slip; "dry scalp feels
comfortable"; "fewer dry flakes"; "the look of fuller edges"; "less
breakage on wash day."

**Customer results**: show typical results only, labeled "Results vary."
Disclose when a product was given free. (Note: this is more conservative
than the brand owner's reconfirmed approach to invented testimonial
names in `profile.md` — that decision stands as made; this rule applies
to any *real* customer results/photos used, not to the invented-name
testimonial device.)

**Rosemary vs. minoxidil**: one small 2015 trial exists; it is not proof
— don't use it or imply PFA performs like minoxidil.

**Never** suggest relaxers or synthetic braiding hair cause cancer and
that PFA is the fix. (A real pattern in some competitor/category
advertising — explicitly off-limits here.)

**Secret ingredient**: never name it or guess it.

**Website compliance flag — not just ad copy.** This updated research pass
explicitly calls out that PFA's own homepage currently has testimonials
reading "reverse my baldness" and "longer, stronger" — both direct
violations of the claim rules above (growth/regrowth claims). This is
outside the scope of what this skill generates, but worth the brand
owner's attention: the site itself currently contradicts the honest-
positioning strategy this research recommends.

## 7. Verified real Reddit threads (screenshots pulled Nov 2026)

Two actual threads, confirmed (not search-snippet secondhand) — stronger
evidence than the "unverified ChatGPT search" quotes below.

**Thread 1, "Dandruff help!" (~Aug 2026)**: OP tried Sulfur 8 ("it'll work
for a bit but then it'll get really bad"), hair "never dry, it's always
oiled," dandruff persists even right after washing, tried Head & Shoulders
but it stopped working, Royal oils worked for a while. Top reply (35
upvotes): "stop the oiling (on the scalp. putting oil on your hair is
fine! but focus on hydration, oil is great for locking in hydration)."
Reply (18 upvotes): "The oil is quite literally feeding the bad bacteria."
Reply (13 upvotes): "Oil doesn't moisture or hydrate hair it's just a
sealant that shouldn't get on your scalp." Reply (26 upvotes): dandruff
stopped "after 3 months" of more water, a multivitamin, weekly
scalp-focused washing, and a bonnet dryer.

**Thread 2, breakage after color (~Sep 2026)**: OP's hair "gotten
significantly shorter... due to it constantly being dry and breaking off.
Literally getting CHEWED UP bro." "Every day feels like a step in the
wrong direction." Washes weekly, finger-detangles "in an attempt to keep
SOME strands," "I've been doing the LOC methods with no results." Already
tried: Pattern Beauty, Briogeo, Camille Rose, TGIN, K18, Mielle, ApHogee,
clarifying/chelating shampoos. Reply (43 upvotes): "pattern beauty isnt
that moisturizing" and "stop clarifying every 2 weeks esp if ur hair is
already dry." Reply (14 upvotes): real improvement only came from seeing
someone for regular trims, not a product. Reply (7 upvotes): "Oils is not
a moisturizer. Only water can do that. Hair can only take so much oil
before it's full."

**What these threads teach — this sharpens an existing copy rule:**
- **The community firmly believes oil seals moisture but is not itself a
  moisturizer.** PFA copy must agree with this belief, not fight it — see
  the sharpened claim rule below.
- Oiling a flaky/dandruff-prone scalp is widely seen by this community as
  making things *worse*, not better. The flake/dandruff angle works best
  told through real customer experience ("I thought oil would make my
  flakes worse — here's what happened"), not asserted outright.
- "It worked for a while, then it stopped" is its own distinct pain — the
  temporary-fix cycle, separate from "didn't work at all."
- People are drowning in product lists and conflicting advice — a
  recurring, exhausted theme.
- Damaged ends have to be trimmed; people who've accepted that want a
  routine that protects what grows in afterward, not a promise to undo
  existing damage.

**Sharpened claim rule from this finding**: never say PFA "moisturizes"
on its own, or "hydrates the scalp." Say it **seals in** moisture, adds
slip, softens, and adds shine — water/leave-in products moisturize, oil
seals. This is more precise than, and should be used alongside, the
existing "never imply the oil replaces washing/conditioning" rule in
`profile.md`.

(Unverified quotes also appear in the source PDF, sourced from a ChatGPT
search rather than opened threads — e.g. "I've been struggling with
really dry hair for months," "my hair might feel soft for a day, but by
the next day it becomes extremely bone dry," "it was less about the
products and more about routine." Treat these as directionally useful
but not confirmed the way the two threads above are.)

## 8. Realistic results timeline — backed by dermatology sources, with a condition

This updates the earlier blanket "never state a specific results
timeline" guidance with a more precise rule: a cosmetic-only, properly
hedged timeline **is** usable, grounded in real dermatology sources, but
**only when backed by real customer check-in data** (see below) — don't
just assert these weeks from the research alone without customer proof
behind the specific ad.

**The science behind it** (background only — never put the raw stats in
an ad): scalp hair grows about half an inch/month on average (American
Academy of Dermatology / Dr. Hadley King). Afro-textured hair specifically
grows slower than other textures in measured studies (~256 micrometers/
day vs. 396 for Caucasian hair — roughly 3.5–4 inches/year) — **this is
for internal honesty about timelines only; never say "your hair grows
slower" in an ad, since that corrects the reader's texture, which
`brand-book.md`'s "celebrate, never correct" rule forbids.** Shedding
50–100 hairs/day is normal (reassurance material, not a claim). Oils
measurably lower combing force and can enhance shaft lubrication
(Gavazzoni Dias, 2015) — real, citable support for the slip/breakage
claims already in use. A 2022 systematic review of 22 studies (Phong et
al.) found weak evidence oils improve luster and no strong evidence
castor or argan oil grows hair — coconut oil has the best evidence, and
PFA doesn't contain coconut oil, so don't borrow coconut oil's results by
implication.

**Timeline PFA can stand behind** (cosmetic results only, from consistent
use 2–3 times/week):
- **First wash day**: more slip while detangling, softer and shinier
  hair.
- **Weeks 1–2**: scalp feels comfortable between washes, fewer dry
  flakes.
- **Weeks 4–8**: less breakage on wash day, fewer snapped ends in the
  comb.
- **Month 3 and on**: holding onto more of the length already being
  grown — "length is the long game," never "faster growth."

**Before any ad states a specific week, back it with real customer
check-in data** — ask 5–10 real customers for a photo and a one-line note
at day 1, week 4, week 8, and week 12 (same lighting/angle each time).
Label results with the person's real name and "Results vary," disclose
if the bottle was given free, and capture how it *felt* first ("softer
on wash day") before what they saw. This is an operational ask for the
brand owner's actual customer outreach, not something this skill can
generate — flagging it here since it's the prerequisite for using the
timeline above in a specific, confident way rather than a vague one.

## 9. How to sound like the customer — voice guide with ready lines

**Patterns in how real customers actually talk:**
- **In moments, not categories**: "day 3 of braids," "wash day,"
  "takedown," "by Wednesday it's dry," "my edges after my install" — not
  "during the hair care routine."
- **About past failures, by name and feeling**: "the peppermint one
  burned," "it just sat on top of my hair," "greasy," "built up," "didn't
  do anything."
- **Skeptically, and admitting it**: "I know, another oil" — naming the
  skepticism earns more trust than ignoring it.
- **First person, short, plain**: never "nourishing botanical blend" —
  "my hair actually felt soft."
- **About their own hair with pride, even when frustrated**: they're mad
  at the *products*, never at their own hair — directly reinforces
  `brand-book.md`'s "celebrate, never correct" rule from the customer's
  own voice, not just as a brand mandate.

**Words that sound like them** (use freely): wash day, slip, detangle,
takedown, edges, buildup, greasy, weighed down, it burned, didn't do
anything, dry by day two, my scalp was itching, it actually felt soft.

**Words that sound like an ad, avoid** (adds to the brand-voice
words-to-avoid list in `profile.md`/`brand-book.md`): transform,
game-changer, luxurious, revolutionary, and any specific fast-timeline
promise like "results in 14 days." (Miracle, unruly, tame, manageable,
frizz-free, abundance, worthy are already banned — this list confirms
them independently and adds transform/game-changer/luxurious/
revolutionary/specific-day-count claims as new.)

**Ready-to-use example lines (already compliant, already sentence case —
good calibration reference):**
- "If you've got a drawer full of oils that didn't do anything, we get
  it."
- "We're not going to promise you inches in two weeks. Nobody honest
  can."
- "It won't change your hair overnight. Your next wash day will feel
  different, though."
- "No peppermint burn. No 30-day miracle. Just an oil you'll actually
  keep using."
- "Consistency is the whole secret. We just made the oil easy to be
  consistent with."
- "Soft on wash day. Comfortable by week two. The rest is patience."
- "You've tried the hype. Try the one that tells you the truth about
  timing."

**Phrasing lifted directly from real posts (use the phrasing, never the
poster's name or identity)**: "it'll work for a bit but then it'll get
really bad," "it stopped working," "literally getting chewed up," "every
day feels like a step in the wrong direction," "trying to keep SOME
strands," "my hair's always oiled but still dry," "I did the LOC method
with no results," "a routine that's better for my new growth."

**A reusable 5-step structure for "tried it, didn't work" concepts**
(distinct from, and more specific than, the general Hook→Body→CTA
framework in `profile.md` — best suited to TESTIMONIAL, Prop Testimonial,
and Before/After style concepts):
1. Name the failed attempt in the customer's own words ("the growth oil
   that did nothing").
2. Say honestly why it failed (overpromised, burned, too heavy).
3. Say what PFA does differently, and what it does *not* promise.
4. Give the realistic, hedged timeline (section 8 above).
5. End calm: "Be yourself. Feel plenty."

**Copy rules from this map:**
- Never say PFA "moisturizes" on its own or "hydrates the scalp." Say it
  seals in moisture, adds slip, softens, and adds shine.
- Never pitch "one oil replaces your whole routine." "One blend, four
  ways" means it replaces the reader's *other oils* (pre-wash oil, scalp
  oil, shine serum, edge product) — not water, conditioner, or leave-in.
  This sharpens the existing "Critical copy-accuracy rule" in
  `profile.md` — use this more precise "replaces oils, not the whole
  routine" framing going forward.
- Name the failed attempt in the customer's own words, agree with what
  they learned the hard way, then show the specific step where PFA fits.
- The "worked for a while, then stopped" frustration is real — answer it
  with consistency and honesty, never "this one won't stop working."
