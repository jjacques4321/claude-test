describe the scalp itch in their own words, unprompted.

## 5. Objections to answer (usable as body copy, not just internal notes)

- **"Don't put oil on your scalp."** Answer: a few drops on a clean
  scalp, worked in, two or three times a week. It doesn't replace washing.
- **"Don't oil if you have dandruff."** Answer: agree. PFA is for dry,
  tight scalps that flake from dryness — not dandruff. Greasy yellow
  flakes, redness, or sores need a dermatologist, not an oil. (Good
  trust-building line: redirecting a genuine medical case to a
  dermatologist rather than overselling the product.)
- **"Oils don't grow hair."** Answer: agree, directly. PFA promises
  moisture, slip, shine, and softness — not growth. This honest-
  disagreement framing is itself a strong, differentiated hook
  (most competitors imply growth; PFA can stand out by explicitly not
  claiming it).
- **"Will it burn?"** Answer: no peppermint, no menthol, about 1%
  essential oils.

## 6. Claim rules (supplement to `nia-voice-validation.md`'s DO/DON'T table)

**Never say** (additional banned phrases beyond the other file's list):
"regrows," "grows edges back," "stops hair loss," "dandruff" or
"antidandruff" (stronger than the other file's "treats dandruff" — this
one bans the bare word), "relieves itching," "clinically proven," "works
like minoxidil," "rosemary is proven to grow hair." The FDA treats growth
and dandruff claims as drug claims.

**OK to say**: moisture, shine, softness, slip; "dry scalp feels
comfortable"; "fewer dry flakes"; "the look of fuller edges"; "less
breakage on wash day."

**Customer results**: show typical results only, labeled "Results vary."
Disclose when a product was given free. (Note: this is more conservative
than the brand owner's reconfirmed approach to invented testimonial
names in `profile.md` — that decision stands as made; this rule applies
to any *real* customer results/photos used, not to the invented-name
testimonial device.)

**Rosemary vs. minoxidil**: one small 2015 trial exists; it is not proof
— don't use it or imply PFA performs like minoxidil.

**Never** suggest relaxers or synthetic braiding hair cause cancer and
that PFA is the fix. (A real pattern in some competitor/category
advertising — explicitly off-limits here.)

**Secret ingredient**: never name it or guess it.
