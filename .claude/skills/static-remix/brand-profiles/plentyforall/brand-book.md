# PlentyForAll Brand Book — voice, visual identity, official usage guide

Sourced from the official "PlentyForAll Brand Guidelines" PDF (2026, by
Prescillia Pichonnier). This is the authoritative source for brand
voice/tone, color, typography, and visual direction — distinct from the
three audience/claims research files, which are about who to target and
what's legally safe to say. This file is about *how the brand sounds and
looks* regardless of who the concept targets.

## Tagline and core values

**Tagline**: "Be yourself. Feel plenty."

- **Inclusivity**: every texture and every form of self-expression
  deserves a place.
- **Authenticity**: the freedom to wear your hair your way and feel at
  ease being yourself.
- **Community**: creating connection through shared experiences while
  embracing individuality.

## Voice & tone

Warm, assured, and lightly playful. Like talking to a friend who knows
hair and makes you feel understood.

- **Confident**: celebrating texture, never preaching how hair should
  look. *"Your hair. Your way."*
- **Welcoming**: room for every texture and story, never just one hair
  type. *"From waves to coils, locs to edges. One oil, plenty for all."*
- **Joyful**: a little wit, without hype or false urgency. *"Hair so good
  you'll need to leave the house."*
- **Knowledgeable**: clear, useful, and honest — never clinical, never
  promising what we can't show. *"For waves, warm 1–2 drops in your palms
  and start at the ends."*

## Writing rules — apply to every concept

1. **Benefit first.** Say what the hair will feel like, then why.
2. **Represent and name every texture.** Waves, curls, coils, and locs.
   Rotate who leads a given piece of content, and say who the oil is for.
3. **Celebrate, never correct.** Texture is never a problem to fix. Never
   use "tame," "unruly," or "frizz-free." This distinction matters for ad
   hooks specifically: it's fine to call out an external problem (tension
   from styling, a routine that isn't working, products that don't
   deliver) — it is **not** fine to frame the customer's own natural
   texture as the thing that's wrong. "Your hair was never the problem,
   breakage was" (already used in a prior concept) is exactly the right
   shape of this distinction; "your hair looks a mess" would violate it.
4. **Specific beats superlative.** "Slip," "softness," and "shine" say
   more than "best," "amazing," or "miracle."
5. **Calm, not loud.** **Sentence case, not Title Case, for headlines.**
   No em dashes. No countdowns/urgency gimmicks. *This is a real
   correction to how most concepts have been written so far* — headlines
   like "What To Do When You've Tried Every Hair Oil And Your Hair Still
   Won't Grow" are Title Case; the brand-correct version is sentence
   case: "What to do when wash day feels like a whole job." Going
   forward, write headlines in sentence case and avoid em dashes in favor
   of periods or simple commas.
6. **Promise what you can show.** Moisture, shine, softness. No growth,
   no regrowth — consistent with the legal claim rules already in this
   profile, now doubly confirmed as a brand-voice choice, not just a
   compliance one.
7. **Let the community speak.** Quote customers and credit creators. (The
   brand's own "real people, real results" example pairs this with real
   before/after photography when it exists — see Visual direction below.
   This doesn't reopen the settled call on invented testimonial names;
   it's additional context that the brand's ideal is real-and-credited
   when real material is available.)
8. **Small details matter.** "PlentyForAll" as one word in copy/handles
   (the logo lockup itself reads "PLENTY FOR ALL" with spaces — that's
   fine for the logo, but written brand references should read
   "PlentyForAll"). 3–5 hashtags including #PlentyForAll. One emoji at
   most.

### Words to avoid (brand-voice reasons, separate from the legal list)

**Frame texture as a problem** — never: tame, unruly, manageable,
frizz-free.
**Too spiritual for the brand** — never: sacred, abundance, worthy.
**Claims the brand can't make** — never: miracle, regrowth, cure,
reverse. (These three overlap with the legal DO/DON'T tables elsewhere in
this profile — treat them as banned for both reasons.)

### Before/after copy examples (straight from the brand book)

- "Made with love and intention" → "A blend of nourishing oils for
  moisture, shine and softness on waves, curls, coils and locs."
- "See yourself. Be Yourself. Feel Plenty." → "Be yourself. Feel plenty."
  (a clean example of the sentence-case correction in rule 5)
- "Grow your edges back!" → "Softer, shinier hair you can feel after one
  wash day."

## Voice in action — tone flexes by content type

- **Education**: clear, step-by-step, encouraging. *"Wash day,
  simplified. Save this for Sunday."*
- **Community**: celebratory, always crediting the person. *"Look at
  those coils. Thank you for sharing, @name."*
- **Product**: benefit first and specific. *"A rich blend for moisture
  and slip, without the heavy feel."*
- **Texture spotlight**: specific, respectful, rotating so every texture
  gets its moment. *"Locs need moisture too. A few drops along the
  parts, then massage. Twice a week is plenty."*

## Official product usage — supersedes the earlier 3-use summary

**"One blend. Four ways. Every texture."** This is the brand's own usage
framework (matches the four uses already used to structure the 12-avatar
table in `pfa-research-stats-avatars.md` — that file's "pre-wash, scalp
massage, finishing oil, edge care" categories come from here).

**Four ways to use it:**
1. **Pre-wash treatment** — work through dry lengths, section by section.
   Leave on 20–30 minutes, then shampoo as usual.
2. **Scalp massage** — apply along the parts with the nozzle tip, massage
   with fingertips for 2–3 minutes, before wash day or between washes.
3. **Define & shine finishing oil** — on dry hair, warm a few drops in
   your palms and scrunch to break the gel cast, or lightly glaze over
   the length to refresh curl definition and add shine.
4. **Edge care** — apply 1 drop onto edges with a fingertip or brush.
   Conditions hair and adds shine.

**Texture-specific application** (useful for precise, credible how-to
copy):
- **Waves**: warm 1–2 drops between palms, smooth over the ends only,
  skip the roots — a little goes a long way. Best for: finishing oil,
  pre-wash.
- **Curls & coils**: warm 3–4 drops between palms, glide through the
  lengths section by section. Start with less for fine/looser curls;
  tighter, denser coils can take more. Best for: finishing oil, pre-wash.
- **Locs**: apply a few drops along the parts with the nozzle tip, then
  massage into the scalp. Light touch on the locs themselves to avoid
  buildup. Best for: scalp massage, finishing oil.

This is more precise than, and should be used in place of, the earlier
generic "pre-poo / LOC-LCO sealing / standalone scalp treatment" summary
when a concept needs specific how-to copy.

## Color palette (official, full)

| Name | Hex | Character |
|---|---|---|
| Teal | #05A79C | Freshness and energy — the primary accent (matches what's already used) |
| Navy | #003C64 | Depth |
| Charcoal | #1C1C1C | Depth/grounding |
| Forest Green | #2B402B | Depth |
| Mustard | #D9A441 | Warmth and personality |
| Cream | #F1ECE4 | Breathing room, background |

Overall palette character: **expressive, grounded, and confident.**
Previous concepts have used teal almost exclusively as the accent color —
**the brand has a fuller palette.** Pull in navy, forest green, mustard,
and cream for background/prop color variety across a batch, not just
teal every time; this also helps with the compositional-variety problem
noted in `SKILL.md`/`concept-history.md`.

## Typography

- **League Spartan (Bold)** — accent, heading, button. A bold, geometric
  sans with confident, rounded-square letterforms.
- **DM Sans** — heading, body, button. A clean, warm, slightly rounded
  grotesque.

Nano Banana Pro can't render an exact licensed typeface, but naming these
in a prompt ("bold geometric sans-serif similar to League Spartan" for
headlines, "clean rounded sans-serif similar to DM Sans" for body text)
steers generation closer to the brand's actual type character than a
generic "bold sans-serif" instruction.

## Visual direction

Warm, expressive visuals built around **personality, texture, and
connection.** Close portraits sit alongside candid group moments —
laughter, movement, the confidence of wearing your hair your way. Tight
crops and unexpected angles bring energy and intimacy. A soft, grainy,
film-inspired finish keeps imagery relaxed and familiar, not polished-
corporate.

**Inclusivity note — this affects the model-representation rule
elsewhere in this profile.** The brand's own moodboard and "brand in
action" examples show a deliberate mix of ethnicities and hair textures
(waves, curls, coils, locs) together, not exclusively one group. This
doesn't override the audience research — the validated pain points
(edges, scalp itch, traction alopecia, etc.) are specifically
well-evidenced for Black women with coily/kinky hair, and concepts
targeting those specific moments should keep featuring that
representation, since that's where the evidence actually lives. But for
general lifestyle/community/brand-moment content (not tied to a specific
researched pain point), the brand's own visual identity wants broader
representation across textures and ethnicities, and explicitly says to
**"rotate who leads."** Don't treat "always a Black woman with 4C/coily
hair" as an absolute rule for every single concept — match the model to
the specific avatar/moment the concept is actually depicting.

**Real customer photography**: the brand's own "brand in action" examples
include genuine before/after photos labeled "Real people. Real results."
This is the brand's established pattern for real photographic proof when
real material exists — separate from, and not a reopening of, the
settled decision on invented testimonial *text* quotes.
